Work / Officeworks

Banner blindness

Banners are a critical way of communicating company initiatives, tools and business partnerships. Click-through rates were below 1% across the entire Officeworks website, and our team was asked how we might improve communication through banner placement and design.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Company
Wesfarmers Group — Officeworks
Services
UX research, A/B testing, Design system templating, Outcomes reporting
  1. Problem
  2. Solution
  3. Production

Problem

Using Hotjar heatmaps, we observed that Officeworks users were not engaging with key content. I formed the hypothesis that users perceived much of the content as advertising and were therefore ignoring it. Across the website, UI elements and competing content all fought for users’ attention.

Users were often not browsing casually, but visiting the site to complete a specific task. Over time they had developed learned behaviours that prioritised elements perceived as useful — navigation, search and headlines — while ignoring content considered low value. Promotional banners became one of the strongest examples of this behaviour.

Observations

Analysis of over 300 banners, current and historical, showed a pattern among high-engagement banners: they all had minimal graphics and a simple message.

Key findings

Banners that don’t look like advertising have more credibility (higher click-through rates) and promote trust within a branded system.

Context. To understand context, I visited Officeworks stores to see how in-store initiatives were being advertised.

Solution

The first logical step was to define what specifically made something look like advertising to Officeworks customers. We did this through semi-structured user interviews. The results:

  • Small rectangular shapes in the middle of images.
  • Multiple typefaces.
  • Coloured backgrounds.
  • Text embedded over an image.

How might we design a banner that avoids all of the above — and how would it perform?

With statistical significance, our hypothesis was proven: users were ignoring banners that looked like ads. Neutral tones and simplified messaging were more effective at communicating business partnerships, tools and initiatives, and the improved brand consistency increased brand credibility.

Production

To ensure correct delivery across multiple areas of the business, I wrote extensive documentation for both designers and engineers. The design rules fed into a banner builder that automated banner creation, so every merchandise team could generate HTML banners in minutes, not days.
